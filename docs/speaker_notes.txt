SNN and ANN Presentation Speaker Notes

Marcus Vu - slides 2, 3, 4 part 1, 5, 6 and 10

Slide 2  Table of contents

- Roadmap: the energy problem, dataset, models, project changes, results and conclusions.

Slide 3  Problem

- Larger neural networks require more computation and energy.
- We ask whether spike-based processing can achieve similar accuracy at a lower cost.

Slide 4 Part 1  CPU and GPU hardware

- Moving weights and activations between memory and processing adds energy cost.
- Dense GPU operations and repeated SNN time steps can limit savings from sparse spikes.

Slide 5  Dataset and preprocessing

- CIFAR-10 has 60,000 small color images in ten classes, split into 50,000 training and 10,000
  test images.
- Normalize the images and augment training with random crops, flips, color jitter and
  rotations. Both models use the same preprocessing.

Slide 6  Model architecture

- Both networks share a compact convolutional structure to keep model capacity similar.
- The CNN makes one pass. The SNN uses spiking neurons, temporal normalization (TEBN) and four
  time steps.

Slide 10  Changes and enhancements

- A smaller model and a 30-epoch training budget make the paper's approach practical for this
  project.
- Compare measured GPU costs with separate energy estimates from operation counts.
- My contribution makes the notebook easier to run on local NVIDIA GPUs, including Windows.
